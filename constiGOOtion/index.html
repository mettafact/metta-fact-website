Preamble: A Brief History of the Dirty Dozen

Brigdon Foster founded this league. When he founded it is the open question.

The written record begins in 2011, with a ten-team ESPN league called Bucs Nation. It was not the first season. There was a season in 2010 (and allegedly 2009 in freaking Yahoo! of all places), but little beyond rumor survives from either. Brigdon would like to claim an additional title here, but until proof is provided that history is lost. Turns out the internet is not forever.

The league grew to twelve teams in 2013, became Tampa's Dirty Dozen in 2014, jumped to Sleeper in 2019, and went superflex in 2025 — where Anthony Palermo showed up for his first season and immediately won the title, which nobody wants to talk about.

Along the way: Brigdon has won five championships and would like you to know that. He also founded the thing, ran it for years, handed the gavel to Metta, and never fully let go of it — which is how he came to be known as The Shadow. Zac won the 2022 title from a 6-8 regular season, which remains the greatest heist in league history. Billy hung 298.94 in 2019, still the record. And Andrew has played all fifteen seasons under five different names (Tbmeetsatl → TheChessMaster → FullTilt → MettaVerse → Hail_the_King), made the playoffs seven times, and has never once reached the finals. The rebrand is not the problem.

Seven Sleeper seasons, seven different champions. Nobody has repeated since the move. This document exists so that we finally have a way to settle things in group chat wars.

1. What This Is

1.1. These are the rules of the Tampa Dirty Dozen. Pay your dues, take a roster, and you've agreed to all of it.

1.2. If this book is silent on something, the commissioner rules and writes it down in the precedent log (Section 11). If the book and Sleeper's settings disagree, the book wins and the settings get fixed.

1.3. No retroactive rules. Whatever's in effect at kickoff of Week 1 rides for the whole season.

2. Who's in Charge

2.1. The commissioner runs the platform, collects the money, sets the calendar, and settles disputes. Lineage: Brigdon (2011–mid-2010s, exact end date classified) → Andrew "Metta" (2018 at the latest–present). Shadow governments hold no official authority under this document, regardless of how many titles they've won.

2.2. The commissioner also has a team, which is an obvious problem. So: any ruling that materially helps or hurts the commissioner's own squad gets decided by a vote of the other eleven, not by him. His trades get special handling too (7.5).

2.3. Changing the rules — the Draft Day Session. Rule changes are proposed and voted live at the draft, before pick 1. No advance notice required. Walk in with an idea, pitch it to the room, and find out. Passage requires a simple majority: 7 of 12. Remote drafters vote in the chat in real time; anyone unreachable at vote time is an abstention. Passed changes take effect immediately for the season being drafted — which is exactly why the vote happens before pick 1, not after.

2.4. Mid-season rule changes require all 12 of us to agree, which is to say they basically never happen. Broken rules get fixed for next year, not in the middle of a fight about them.

2.5. Firing the commissioner takes 8 of the other 11, and the vote has to name the replacement. No coups without a plan.

3. Money

3.1. Dues: $200 per team, per season, via Venmo or Zelle, in full by draft day. Twelve teams paying in makes $2,400 collected — what becomes prize money is defined in 3.3.

3.2. Don't pay, don't play:

  • Not paid by draft day → you forfeit your final-round pick. You draft normally otherwise. Mechanically: the commissioner drafts a placeholder in your last slot and immediately drops him, and you open the season a roster spot short. (Yes, Sleeper lets us do this. It happens live, in front of everyone.) Forfeitures from multiple violations stack upward by round — see 5.3.
  • Still not paid by Week 1 → -$25 FAAB.
  • Still not paid by Week 4 → the seat gets offered to someone who answers texts.
  • Won money but owe money? Winnings are held until you're square.

3.3. The prize pot is total dues minus the cost of draft weekend (Airbnb, draft board, supplies). Food and drink are separate — see 3.3d. The commissioner posts both figures each year. Whatever's left pays out in these shares:

CategoryShare
Champion40%
Runner-up25%
Third place14%
Highest score, each regular-season week (14 weeks)14% total — 1% per week
Most points, full regular season7%

3.3a. Rounded to whole dollars, with any rounding remainder going to the champion.

3.3b. Weekly high score is awarded for regular-season weeks 1–14 only. Playoff weeks don't count — playoff teams already have something to play for, and the weekly pot exists precisely so the eliminated half of the league keeps setting real lineups in November. A manager wins it however many times he earns it.

3.3c. Most points is total points for across weeks 1–14, regardless of record. The 13-1 team and the 4-10 team are on the same footing here.

3.3d. Draft weekend money, in two buckets.

  • Dues cover the fixed costs: Airbnb, draft board, supplies. These come off the top before payouts are calculated, and whatever isn't spent goes back into the prize pot — spend less on the house, play for more money.
  • Food and drink are on the people who show up, split among live attendees and topped up by remote-draft tolls. This never touches the prize pot in either direction.

3.4. Money in must equal money out, in both buckets. The commissioner posts the draft-weekend accounting after the draft and the prize-pot ledger before the playoffs, so nobody has to wonder about either.

4. The Format

4.1. 12 teams. Half-PPR (0.5/catch, 4-pt passing TDs). Lineup: QB, 2 RB, 2 WR, TE, 2 FLEX, Superflex. No kickers, no defenses — we're not doing that anymore. Five bench spots (down from six, effective 2026), plus 2 IR slots.

4.1a. IR eligibility: players designated Out, Suspended, NA, or DNR/holdout/opt-out may occupy an IR slot. Doubtful players may not — doubtful guys sometimes play, and allowing them turns IR into a Sunday-morning free bench spot. When a player's designation clears, he comes off IR immediately; if there's no room, someone gets dropped.

4.2. The full scoring table — including the nonstandard stuff — lives in Appendix C and is the written record of what Sleeper should say. If Sleeper's settings ever drift from Appendix C, the appendix wins and the settings get fixed. Changing any of it requires a Draft Day Session vote (2.3), after which the appendix gets updated to match.

5. The Draft

5.1. When the draft happens. For 2026: Saturday, August 29. From 2027 on, the draft is held on the last Saturday in August that falls on or before the 22nd — which lands between the 16th and the 22nd every year, and is always the third Saturday except in years when August begins on a Saturday. Put it on the calendar in January and stop asking.

5.1a. Draft order — the Annual Spectacle. By long tradition, something stupid is invented every year to set the order. That tradition is preserved and protected, and responsibility for it belongs to The Shadow — a job with no authority, no pay, and total creative control, which suits him. Three conditions on it:

  • The outcome is decided by chance, not skill. Dice, blind draws, a horse race, numbered cans in a cooler, somebody's kid pulling names out of a hat — all fair game. Contests that reward preparation, football knowledge, or reading the rules first are not, because whoever designs the thing is also playing in it.
  • The method and its full rules are announced to everyone before anyone competes, and don't change once announced.
  • If any part of it requires a judgment call, a non-commissioner makes that call.

If The Shadow produces nothing by the week before draft day, the order is the Sleeper randomizer, run live in front of everyone. That's the fallback, not a punishment — a plain random draw is a perfectly honest way to do this, and any year the spectacle feels like more trouble than it's worth, take it.

5.2. Show up — in person or online, both count. In person is the goal; drafting live through the app from wherever you are is completely fine, but remote drafters pay a $20 toll toward the food and drink fund (3.3d) — consider it your bar tab for the draft party you skipped. If you can't attend either way, send a named proxy (an actual human, cleared with the commissioner beforehand) to pick for you; the toll applies. The bar here is "a person with a pulse made your picks live."

5.3. No-shows — no attendance, no remote login, no proxy, just autodraft doing its thing:

  • You forfeit your final-round pick (same placeholder-and-drop mechanic as 3.2), and start the season a roster spot short.
  • Forfeitures stack. If you've already lost your final-round pick under another rule, the next forfeiture takes your second-to-last pick, then the round above that. Manage to owe three and you're speedrunning Section 10.
  • Two no-shows in a row means you've quit (Section 10). We'll miss you.

5.4. Real technical failures during the draft get a pause, up to 15 minutes. "I fell asleep" is not a technical failure.

5.5. Keepers. Each team may keep up to two players from its final previous-season roster. Keeping is optional — zero, one, or two.

5.6. What a keeper costs.

  • First kept season: one round higher than where he was drafted. Drafted in the 6th → he costs your 5th. Undrafted players (waiver/FAAB pickups) cost a 7th-round pick.
  • Second kept season: one round higher again. That 5th becomes a 4th.
  • Costs cap at the 1st round. There's nowhere above round 1 to climb — a 1st-round pick costs a 1st both years, and a 2nd-rounder whose cost hits the 1st stays there for his second season.
  • Term limit: two kept seasons, maximum. After that he re-enters the draft pool for everybody, no exceptions, not even your superflex QB.

Worked example: you draft a guy in the 6th in 2026. He costs your 5th in 2027, your 4th in 2028, and in 2029 he's back in the pool with everyone else.

5.7. Keeper mechanics. Keeper declarations are due 2 weeks before the draft and the full league-wide list is published immediately — everyone drafts knowing who's off the board. If your two keepers would cost the same round, the second one moves up a round. Keeper rights travel with the player: trade for a guy mid-season and you inherit his keeper cost, history and all.

5.8. Keepers and traded picks. A keeper is a pick — so you need to still own the pick his cost lands on. Trade away your 2027 5th, and any keeper who'd cost a 5th that year can't be kept. That's not a gotcha, it's the whole strategic point of allowing pick trades: when you deal a pick during the season, you're also spending a keeper slot you might have wanted in the offseason. Trade with that in mind. (If a keeper's round is unavailable, he simply goes back in the pool — he does not slide to a different round.)

5.9. First keeper season: 2027, based on players drafted or picked up in 2026. Everyone drafts this season knowing the rules. Nobody gets retroactive lottery tickets on a 2025 roster they built blind.

5.10. Administrative note: the league is currently configured on Sleeper as Redraft. Before the 2027 season it must be switched to Keeper for any of §5.5–5.9 to function. That's the commissioner's job and it happens at renewal, not on draft day.

6. Waivers

6.1. $100 FAAB for the season. FAAB is tradeable in whole dollars as part of any trade. Note for the record: despite being nominally available, FAAB has never actually changed hands in a trade in league history — every trade on the books moved players and picks only. Consider this an invitation. Sleeper handles this natively — budget is added inside the trade builder, so there's no setting to switch on.

6.2. How waivers actually run. FAAB blind bidding, minimum bid $0. Waivers process daily at 3:00 AM ET, Monday through Saturday. Sunday is open free agency — first come, first served, no bidding, so Sunday morning inactives are a race.

6.2a. Dropped players go to waivers for 1 day, not straight to free agency. This closes the Sunday loophole: without it, a manager can cut a player Sunday morning and have him instantly claimable by whoever's watching — which turns into accidental gifts at best and arranged handoffs at worst. Under this rule a Sunday drop clears at 3:00 AM Monday, through the normal FAAB process, where everyone gets a fair shot at him.

6.3. Lost a bid by a dollar? Tragic. The commissioner doesn't reverse waiver results unless Sleeper itself broke.

7. Trades

7.1. Deadline: end of Week 12. Moved back from Week 10, where it sat every season from 2019 through 2025 — the extra two weeks give contenders and sellers a real window before the playoffs. After that, nothing moves until the offseason.

7.2. The only reviewable sin is collusion. Two teams funneling assets one direction, an eliminated team gifting its roster to a buddy, anything involving side payments outside the league — that's reviewable. A trade that's merely stupid is not. Bad trades are a league tradition and they stand. And to be explicit, since a good number of us are related: being related to your trade partner is not evidence of collusion. Family trades get the same collusion-only review as everyone else's — no more, no less.

7.3. Trades sit in a 1-day review window (Sleeper's configured setting). Default is approval. If the commissioner smells actual collusion, he freezes it, tells both parties exactly what the evidence is, and rules within 48 hours.

7.4. Blocked trade? Appeal to the league. 8 of 12 (the traders can vote) overturns the block.

7.5. The commissioner's own trades skip his review entirely. They process automatically after the window unless 8 of 12 vote to block under the same collusion-only standard. Anyone can call for that vote. This is the price of the commissioner also fielding a team.

7.6. Proven collusion: trade reversed. Do it twice and Section 10 has a conversation with you.

7.7. Draft picks are tradeable — current-year picks any time before the draft, and picks one season ahead during the season (pre-deadline). No trading picks two-plus years out; this is a keeper league, not a dynasty rebuild simulator. Traded picks attach to the franchise, not the person: if a manager leaves, the seat's incoming and outgoing picks convey to the replacement exactly as they stand. (Money debts don't transfer — see 10.3 — but pick obligations do. You're buying the team, liens included.)

8. Set Your Lineup

8.1. Every week, every manager fields a legal, honest lineup. No empty slots, no bye-week guys, no players ruled Out before Sunday morning when you've got a healthy body on the bench.

8.2. If you're alive for the playoffs or your losses affect who makes it, you play it straight through Week 14. Tanking on purpose is a violation. Losing because you're bad is protected by this constitution.

8.3. First offense: a warning. Second: -$20 FAAB. Full-on ghost mode: Section 10. The commissioner never touches another team's lineup — see 10.2 for the one exception.

9. Playoffs

9.1. Six teams, Week 15 start, championship Week 17. Top two seeds get byes. One week per round — no two-week championship.

9.1a. No re-seeding. Teams stay on their initial side of the bracket the whole way, so the path you draw in Week 15 is the path you get, even if higher seeds fall on the other side.

9.1b. The bottom six play the Toilet Bowl. Losers advance. The team that loses its way to the bottom is crowned 💩 King and shall be addressed as such until the following season's draft.

9.2. Seeding ties break the way Sleeper computes them: record → points for → points against. Head-to-head is not used, however much you want it to be — the platform doesn't apply it, and a constitution that says otherwise is a constitution nobody can enforce. If two teams are still dead even after all three, the commissioner flips a coin on a recorded video call and manually sets the seed.

9.3. In-game playoff ties: the higher seed advances. This is Sleeper's default and there's no setting to change it. Earn the better seed.

9.4. Week 18 exists for the NFL, not for us. It counts for nothing and stays out of the record book.

10. Quitting, Ghosting, and Replacements

10.1. Unresponsive for 14 days in-season, two straight draft no-shows, or dues still unpaid after Week 4 (the same point where 3.2 opens the seat) — that's abandonment.

10.2. The commissioner babysits an abandoned team at minimum power: best-projected legal lineup, no adds, no trades. If a lineup call on that team could help the commissioner's own squad, two neutral managers make it instead.

10.3. Replacements get recruited by the commissioner and approved by a simple majority. They inherit the roster and remaining FAAB. They do not inherit the debts or the shame.

11. Disputes and the Precedent Log

11.1. Got a beef? Put it in writing. The commissioner restates both sides fairly, cites the section, rules, and posts the reasoning to the league — not just the verdict.

11.2. Every ruling goes in the league records with date, issue, ruling, and why. Logged rulings are binding precedent until a vote overrides them. We are building case law about fake football.

11.3. If the book is silent, the commissioner says so out loud, rules for the case in front of him, and proposes real language for the next offseason vote.

12. Amending This Thing

12.1. Adopted by 7 of 12. Amended by 7 of 12 at the Draft Day Session only (2.3–2.4). Keeper-rule changes voted at a draft apply starting the following season — you don't get to rewrite keeper costs in the same room where you're about to use them.

12.2. The current version lives in the league records project. Every amendment gets logged: season, change, vote, reason.
